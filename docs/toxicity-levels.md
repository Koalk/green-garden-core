# What the toxicity levels mean

Green Garden grades every plant's effect on each group of people and animals from 0 to 3. The card shows
the highest level of any group; opening it lists every group with its own level. This is a general
guide in our own words, built from the facts in the catalogue (which come from the sources named in each
entry); it is not medical or veterinary advice.

| Level | Name | Pets (cats, dogs, small pets) | People (children, adults) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | none | No effect known in the sources read. | No effect known in the sources read. |
| 1 | mild | Drooling, a sore mouth or an upset stomach; usually passes by itself. | A sore mouth, skin irritation or an upset stomach; usually passes by itself. |
| 2 | moderate | Marked pain or swelling, or repeated vomiting. Call a vet or an animal poison line. | Marked pain or swelling, or repeated vomiting. Call a doctor or a poison line. |
| 3 | severe | Can harm the heart, nerves or organs. An emergency: go to a vet at once. | Can harm the heart, nerves or organs. An emergency: get medical help at once. |

Notes:

- The level is the worst case over the groups. A plant that is level 1 for dogs and level 3 for cats shows
  level 3 on the card; the list underneath says which is which.
- A plant that has not been assessed shows "not assessed", never "safe". Level 0 means no effect is known,
  not that the plant is safe to eat: any plant material can upset a stomach.
- Small pets (rabbits, rodents, birds) have no systematic source behind them; the catalogue leaves them
  unassessed unless an entry says otherwise.
- The effect depends on the plant part and the amount, and sources differ. If someone has eaten or touched
  a plant and feels unwell, contact a doctor, a vet or a poison line, and name the plant.

The strings are `tox.level.<n>.pets`, `tox.level.<n>.people` and `tox.guide` in
`packages/i18n/src/locales/`. The shared-care text of uses the same wording.
